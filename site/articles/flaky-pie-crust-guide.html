How to Make a Flaky Pie Crust (and Not Be Afraid of It)

By the Front Porch Suppers team · Updated November 22, 2026 · 7 min read

Store-bought crusts are a perfectly good shortcut, and many of our recipes say so. But a homemade crust is easier than its reputation, and the flavor is wonderful. Here is a dependable all-butter crust and the tips that make it work.

All-butter pie crust (one double crust)

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup (2 sticks) very cold butter, cut into 1/2-inch cubes
  • 6 to 8 tablespoons ice water
  1. Whisk the flour, sugar, and salt in a large bowl.
  2. Add the cold butter. Use a pastry cutter or your fingertips to work it in until the pieces are the size of peas, with some larger flattened bits.
  3. Sprinkle in 6 tablespoons of ice water and toss with a fork. Squeeze a handful: if it holds together, it is ready. If it crumbles, add more water a tablespoon at a time.
  4. Divide in half, press each into a flat disk, wrap, and refrigerate at least 1 hour or up to 2 days.

The three secrets of a flaky crust

  • Keep everything cold. Cold butter creates steam pockets in the oven, and that is what makes the layers. If the dough feels soft, chill it for 15 minutes.
  • Do not overwork it. Stop mixing as soon as the dough holds together. Visible bits of butter are a good sign.
  • Let it rest. Chilling relaxes the gluten so the crust does not shrink in the oven.

Rolling and shaping

  • Roll on a lightly floured counter from the center outward, turning the dough a quarter turn every few rolls.
  • Roll to about 12 inches across for a 9-inch pie plate.
  • To move it, roll the dough loosely around the rolling pin and unroll it over the plate.
  • For a single crust, fold the overhang under and crimp with your fingers or press with a fork.

Blind baking (for custard and cream pies)

For pies with a filling that bakes briefly or not at all, bake the crust first: line the chilled crust with parchment, fill with pie weights or dried beans, and bake at 400°F for 20 minutes. Remove the weights and bake 5 to 10 minutes more until golden.

No more soggy bottoms

  • Bake fruit pies on the lowest oven rack, on a preheated baking sheet.
  • Use a glass pie plate so you can see when the bottom is golden.
  • Bake until the filling bubbles thickly in the center. That means the thickener has done its job.
