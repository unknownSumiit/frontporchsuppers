How to Freeze and Reheat Casseroles, Soups, and Leftovers

By the Front Porch Suppers team · Updated November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

A well-stocked freezer is like money in the bank. On a busy day, or a day when you simply do not feel like cooking, a homemade casserole or soup is ready to reheat. Here is how to freeze meals so they taste just as good when you thaw them.

What freezes well

  • Soups, stews, and chili: Broth- and tomato-based soups freeze beautifully. Try our lasagna soup (freeze it without the noodles).
  • Casseroles: Baked pasta dishes like million dollar spaghetti and stuffed cabbage rolls freeze very well.
  • Meatballs and meatloaf: Freeze cooked, in or out of their sauce.
  • Baked goods: Brownies, coffee cake, cornbread, and unfrosted cakes.

What does not freeze well

  • Cream-based soups and sauces can separate (they are still safe, but the texture changes).
  • Cooked potatoes in soup can turn grainy.
  • Mayonnaise-based salads, raw salad greens, and cucumbers.
  • Fried foods lose their crispness.

How to freeze

  1. Cool quickly. Divide hot food into shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours, then freeze once cold.
  2. Wrap well. Use freezer-safe containers or heavy freezer bags, pressing out as much air as possible. For casseroles, cover with plastic wrap and then foil.
  3. Portion smart. Freeze in one- or two-serving portions so you can thaw only what you need.
  4. Label everything. Write the name and date with a marker. Frozen food all looks alike after a month.
  5. Lay bags flat. Soups frozen flat in bags stack neatly and thaw faster.

How long does it keep?

Food kept at 0°F stays safe indefinitely, but quality is best within these times (USDA guidance):

FoodBest quality
Soups and stews2 to 3 months
Casseroles2 to 3 months
Cooked meat and poultry2 to 6 months
Breads, muffins, and cakes2 to 3 months

How to thaw and reheat safely

  • Thaw in the refrigerator, usually overnight. Never thaw on the counter.
  • Reheat to 165°F, measured with a food thermometer in the center.
  • Soups can be reheated straight from frozen in a covered saucepan over low heat, stirring often.
  • Casseroles reheat best covered with foil in a 350°F oven. Allow about twice the normal baking time if baking from frozen.

For more on safe temperatures, see the USDA guide to freezing and food safety.
