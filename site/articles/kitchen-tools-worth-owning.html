10 Kitchen Tools Worth Owning (and a Few You Can Skip)

By the Front Porch Suppers team · Updated November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

You do not need a kitchen full of gadgets to cook well. A handful of good, comfortable tools will handle nearly every recipe on this site. Here are the ones we reach for most, chosen for everyday usefulness and ease of use.

The essentials

  1. A sharp chef’s knife. A sharp knife is safer than a dull one because it does not slip. Choose one that feels comfortable and balanced in your hand.
  2. A large cutting board with a non-slip base or a damp towel underneath.
  3. An instant-read thermometer. The single best tool for food safety and perfectly cooked meat. Look for a large, backlit display.
  4. A 10-inch cast-iron or heavy nonstick skillet for searing, sautéing, and skillet cornbread.
  5. A Dutch oven (5 to 6 quarts) for soups, stews, and braises. Enameled versions are easy to clean.
  6. Rimmed baking sheets (half-sheet size) for roasting, baking cookies, and sheet cakes.
  7. A 9 by 13-inch baking dish for casseroles, cobblers, and baked French toast.
  8. Measuring cups and spoons with large, easy-to-read markings.
  9. A good vegetable peeler with a wide, soft-grip handle.
  10. Long tongs and a sturdy spatula that keep your hands away from heat.

Comfort and safety extras

  • Silicone oven mitts that cover the forearm.
  • A jar opener or rubber grip pad for stubborn lids.
  • An electric can opener if hand openers are hard on your wrists.
  • A kitchen timer with a loud alarm, or use the timer on your phone or smart speaker.
  • A sturdy step stool with a handrail for reaching high shelves.

Tools you can probably skip

  • Single-purpose gadgets like avocado slicers and egg separators. A knife and your hands work fine.
  • Large knife sets. Three good knives (chef’s, paring, and serrated) cover nearly everything.
  • Appliances you will use once a year. Borrow them instead.

Care tips

Hand-wash good knives and dry them right away. Season cast iron by wiping it with a thin coat of oil after washing and drying. Replace worn cutting boards with deep grooves, which can be hard to clean.
